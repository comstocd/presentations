FXI Inhibitors: A Tale of Two Trials

Factor XI inhibitors promise to uncouple thrombosis from hemostasis — the holy grail of anticoagulation. Two Phase 3 trials reported opposite results within months of each other. This session asks why, and what it means for the 3–4 million Americans currently deemed ineligible for anticoagulation.

Key NEJM trials

Narrative Arc

  • Act I — Phase 2 Promise (slides 1–13)

    Opening clinical case. Coagulation cascade as a drug target map. Hemophilia C as the human proof-of-concept. FXI vs. FXIa inhibition — the mechanistic distinction that determined which drug succeeded. Phase 2 venographic DVT data and its limitations.

  • Act II — Reality Check: OCEANIC-AF (slides 14–15)

    Asundexian (FXIa inhibitor) vs. apixaban in 14,000 patients with AF. Stopped for inferiority at interim analysis. The drug reduced bleeding but failed stroke prevention. Critical appraisal: noninferiority design, early stopping, dose-kinetics as mechanistic explanation.

  • Act III — The Signal: AZALEA-TIMI 71 (slides 16–19)

    Abelacimab (FXI zymogen inhibitor) vs. rivaroxaban in 1,287 high-bleeding-risk AF patients. Stopped early for overwhelming safety benefit. 67% reduction in major bleeding; near-zero ICH. Critical appraisal: winner's curse, wide efficacy CI, open-label design, adherence.

  • Act IV — The Unanswered Question: LILAC-TIMI 76 (slide 20)

    Phase 3, placebo-controlled. ~1,900 patients unsuitable for any anticoagulation. Primary endpoint: ischemic stroke. Results expected second half of 2026. Until LILAC reports: AZALEA proves abelacimab is safer than rivaroxaban; it does not prove abelacimab prevents stroke.

  • Close — Return to case + structured debate (slides 21–24)

    Reconsidering the opening patient with AZALEA data in hand. Split-room debate: adopt FXI inhibitors as first-line for high-bleeding-risk AF upon approval? Six take-home points.

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain why FXI is mechanistically distinct from FXa and thrombin as an anticoagulation target
  2. Distinguish FXI inhibition from FXIa inhibition and explain why this difference appears to have determined trial outcomes
  3. Critically appraise AZALEA-TIMI 71 and OCEANIC-AF: design, endpoints, early stopping, phase distinctions, and what LILAC-TIMI 76 must still prove
  4. Articulate the class-wide absence of reversal agents and its inpatient management implications
  5. Identify the highest-priority patient populations for FXI inhibitor candidacy

Opening Clinical Case

Case — Keep This Patient in Mind Throughout A 78-year-old woman with paroxysmal AF (CHA₂DS₂-VASc 5), ESRD on hemodialysis, and a hospitalization 4 months ago for a GI bleed from a cecal AVM is admitted with aspiration pneumonia. Her apixaban was held at that admission. Hematology recommended indefinite hold. Cardiology now requests a formal anticoagulation recommendation.

An estimated 3–4 million Americans meet guideline criteria for anticoagulation in AF but are deemed ineligible due to bleeding risk. This patient is not an edge case — she is the target population. The session is structured so that residents revisit her at the end with the trial data in hand and reassess their initial decision.

Key Trials

OCEANIC-AF (NEJM 2024)

ElementDetail
DrugAsundexian 50 mg PO daily (FXIa active-site inhibitor, Bayer)
ComparatorApixaban 5 mg BID (double-blind, double-dummy)
Population~14,000 patients; AF with CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2(M)/≥3(F); 41 countries
DesignNoninferiority for stroke/SE; superiority for major bleeding; median follow-up ~1.8 years
Primary efficacy resultFailed noninferiority for stroke/SE. More strokes in asundexian arm. DSMB stopped for inferiority (not futility).
Primary safety resultMajor bleeding significantly lower with asundexian; ICH and GI bleeding reduced
Take-homeDrug worked as a safer anticoagulant. It failed as an anticoagulant.

AZALEA-TIMI 71 (NEJM 2025)

ElementDetail
DrugAbelacimab 150 mg SC monthly (anti-FXI zymogen monoclonal antibody, Sanofi/TIMI)
ComparatorRivaroxaban 20 mg daily (open-label, blinded endpoint adjudication)
Population1,287 patients; AF CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥3; enriched for high bleeding risk (prior GI bleed, prior ICH, age ≥80, CrCl 30–50, frailty)
DesignPhase 2b; primary endpoint: major or CRNM bleeding (safety). Stroke/SE secondary — not powered for efficacy. Median follow-up ~21 months.
Primary resultHR 0.33 (95% CI 0.21–0.53) for major bleeding. NNT 14 to prevent 1 major/CRNM bleed. ICH: near-zero in abelacimab arm. Stopped early for overwhelming safety benefit.
Efficacy caveatStroke/SE non-inferior but CI 0.58–2.14; only 25 total stroke events. Cannot conclude abelacimab is equivalent to rivaroxaban for stroke prevention.
Take-homeAZALEA proves abelacimab is dramatically safer than rivaroxaban. It does not prove abelacimab prevents stroke. LILAC-TIMI 76 (vs. placebo, Ph3, results expected H2 2026) answers this.
Critical Methodological Point Both trials were stopped early. Trials stopped early for benefit (AZALEA) systematically inflate effect sizes — the winner's curse. HR 0.33 and NNT 14 are likely somewhat optimistic; real-world data will show regression toward the mean. This caveat is taught explicitly in the session and is one of the 17 discussion questions.

Core Mechanistic Distinction

FXI Inhibition (Upstream)

Targets the zymogen before activation. Prevents FXI → FXIa conversion entirely. Complete upstream blockade of the amplification loop. No unactivated FXI remains available.

Example: Abelacimab (monoclonal antibody, SC monthly)

AZALEA-TIMI 71: dramatic bleeding reduction

FXIa Inhibition (Downstream)

Competes at the active site of already-activated enzyme. Leaves unactivated FXI available for activation. Partial suppression dependent on dose and pharmacokinetics. Trough levels may allow residual FXIa activity.

Examples: Asundexian (PO daily), Milvexian (PO BID, in Phase 3)

OCEANIC-AF: failed stroke prevention

This mechanistic distinction — blocking the zymogen versus competing at the active site — appears to have determined which drug class succeeded. Whether OCEANIC-AF's failure was dose-dependent, kinetics-dependent, or target-dependent will be answered by LIBREXIA-AF (milvexian, Phase 3, ongoing).

The Natural Human Experiment: Hemophilia C

Congenital FXI deficiency (Hemophilia C) provides the pharmacological justification for the entire drug class. Unlike hemophilia A and B (which cause spontaneous hemarthroses), FXI-deficient patients have:

Effect on hemostasis
  • No spontaneous bleeding
  • Normal wound healing
  • Bleeding only with surgery/trauma at high-fibrinolytic sites (dental, urologic)
  • Extrinsic pathway intact — primary hemostatic plug preserved
Effect on thrombosis
  • Significantly reduced DVT and PE rates
  • Lower ischemic stroke risk — especially cardioembolic
  • Reduced AF-related thrombus
  • Supported by Israeli population registry data (Salomon et al., Blood 2008)

Reversal — A Critical Inpatient Gap

Class-Wide — No Exceptions No reversal agents exist for any FXI inhibitor. This is a non-negotiable consideration for in-hospital prescribing and must be part of every informed consent conversation.
  • Andexanet alfa: Designed for FXa inhibitors only — irrelevant here
  • Idarucizumab: Dabigatran-specific — irrelevant here
  • 4F-PCC (Kcentra): Not established; may not overcome mAb-mediated FXI inhibition
  • FFP: Likely ineffective for abelacimab; possibly partial for small molecules
  • rFVIIa (NovoSeven): Theoretical bypass via extrinsic pathway; no clinical evidence
  • Abelacimab t½ = 30 days: Drug effect persists 3+ months post-dose

For life-threatening bleeding: supportive care and source control are primary. Consult hematology early. Management will be empirical. The proportionality argument — abelacimab's near-elimination of ICH means the most dangerous bleeding complication is largely removed — is addressed explicitly in the session.

Patient Selection Framework

PriorityPopulationRationale
Tier 1 AF + prior major GI bleed AZALEA-TIMI 71 enrichment population; LILAC-TIMI 76 tests abelacimab vs. placebo in this group. 93% reduction in GI bleeding vs. rivaroxaban.
Tier 1 AF + prior ICH Current standard is no anticoagulation; near-zero ICH with abelacimab could be practice-changing for this previously unmanageable population.
Tier 1 AF + ESRD No renal clearance; no dose adjustment uncertainty. Addresses the most complex DOAC dosing scenario in clinical practice.
Tier 2 AF + frailty / high fall risk; AF + thrombocytopenia; VTE prophylaxis post-arthroplasty High bleeding susceptibility where DOAC benefit-risk is marginal or unfavorable.
Tier 3 Mechanical heart valves; APS; cancer-associated thrombosis; AF + mild-moderate CKD No data (mechanical valves likely contraindicated); adequate DOAC options available (mild CKD).

Clinical Pearls

🧬The mechanism is real. Hemophilia C proves FXI inhibition reduces thrombosis with minimal spontaneous bleeding. Decades of population registry data support the concept. The pharmacological rationale is sound regardless of what any individual trial shows.
⚠️FXI ≠ FXIa. OCEANIC-AF's failure and AZALEA-TIMI 71's bleeding signal together suggest that complete upstream FXI blockade (abelacimab) may be mechanistically superior to FXIa active-site competition (asundexian). But stroke efficacy awaits LILAC-TIMI 76.
📊AZALEA's bleeding data is compelling. Its efficacy data is hypothesis-generating. HR 0.33 and near-zero ICH vs. rivaroxaban is extraordinary. But 25 total stroke events with a CI of 0.58–2.14 cannot establish non-inferiority to apixaban for stroke. These are not the same claim.
⏱️Early stopping inflates effect size. NNT 14 and HR 0.33 are likely optimistic. Real-world data will show regression toward the mean. This is a feature of trial design, not evidence of fraud — but prescribers should expect real-world performance to be somewhat less dramatic.
🚫No reversal agents — class-wide. Abelacimab's 30-day half-life is the most acute clinical problem. The reversal gap must be part of every prescribing conversation. The counterpoint: near-zero ICH removes the complication that most required reversal.
🔭Two trials will resolve the remaining uncertainty. LILAC-TIMI 76 (abelacimab vs. placebo, Ph3, results H2 2026) answers stroke efficacy. LIBREXIA-AF (milvexian, FXIa, Ph3) answers whether OCEANIC-AF failure was drug-specific or class-specific.
